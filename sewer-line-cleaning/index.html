Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Carrollton, GA

When the main line stops, every drain in the house stops with it. The lowest fixture overflows first, usually a floor drain, shower, or tub. We clear main sewer blockages with cable machines and jetters, then show you what caused them. Call the 24/7 emergency line the moment a drain backs up.

Main line emergency: what to do and what to avoid

Stop using water everywhere in the house. The wastewater from a flush or a shower has nowhere to go, and the lowest drain will take the overflow. Shut off the washing machine and dishwasher first.

Do not use chemical openers in a main line. They do not reach the blockage, they harm pipes, and they injure whoever works on the line next. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell us where water is coming up. If the cleanout in the yard is open and overflowing, say so.

What blocks a main line

Roots are the usual cause in older lines, grease in lines serving kitchens, and wipes in nearly everything. Flushable wipes do not break up the way toilet paper does. They snag on a rough joint and gather other debris until the line closes.

A line can also be blocked by a collapsed section, an offset joint, or a belly that holds water and debris. We clear the blockage first, then camera the line to see whether the pipe itself has a defect.

Which tool clears which blockage

Main lines are bigger than drains, so the equipment is different.

Blockage.Tool.Why.
Roots.Cable machine with a cutting head.Cuts roots out of the joint.
Grease and sludge.Hydro jetter.Scours the wall.
Wipes and paper.Cable machine, sometimes a jetter.Breaks up and flushes the mass.
Collapsed pipe.Camera, then repair.The pipe must be dug up.

How we clear a main line

The procedure is direct.

  1. Open the cleanout.

    We locate the main cleanout and relieve pressure so waste does not flood the house.

  2. Run the machine.

    The cable or jetter goes in until the blockage is reached and cleared.

  3. Flush and test.

    We run water through the line and watch the cleanout to confirm it moves.

  4. Camera if needed.

    If the line has blocked before, we run the camera so you know whether it was a one-time event.

Preventing a repeat in a Carrollton home

Keep wipes, paper towels, feminine products, and grease out of drains. Put a small trash can in each bathroom and a grease can in the kitchen. If you rent near campus and the house has many occupants, post a reminder, because main line blockages grow quickly with heavy use.

Ask us where your main cleanout is and cap it properly. A cleanout that is buried or missing makes any blockage costlier to clear. If your home has none, we can recommend installing one.

What to expect when we arrive

We start by finding the cleanout and checking what is backed up. A technician sets the machine up outside whenever possible so the work does not move through your living space. Floor drains in the basement are covered until we confirm where the blockage sits.

When the line begins to move, wastewater can surge back toward the lowest drain, so we keep the area clear. We run water for several minutes before we call the line clear. If the camera is needed, we do it before we pack up.

Signs the cleaning is not enough

If the same line has needed cleaning three times in a year, the problem is not the blockage. It is the pipe. A bellied section, a root-filled joint, or a collapsed run will keep collecting waste. At that point cleaning only delays the repair.

We explain this plainly when it happens. A camera run costs less than another emergency visit, and it gives you what you need to compare repair options with our sewer line repair page.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. Cleaning a main line is maintenance. A cleanout added during the job is plumbing work the code covers.

Main line work is priced on equipment and access.

  • Whether a cleanout is available or we must pull a toilet.
  • Type of blockage: roots, grease, or debris.
  • Machine required: cable or jetter.
  • Camera inspection afterward.
