Close the main shutoff if water is flowing and cannot be stopped at a fixture. Tell staff and customers to stay out of the wet area, and put up a sign. Wet floors and sewage are safety hazards in any building people use.

Backflow prevention in Carrollton

The City of Carrollton's utility page says commercial accounts handle backflow prevention installation and testing, and it gives a fax number and mailing address for test sheets. Backflow assemblies keep water that has been in a building from reversing into the public supply.

We can help with the plumbing that surrounds an assembly, such as repairs and replacement. Annual testing must be done by someone qualified to test and report, so ask the city who is approved before scheduling. We will tell you what we can do and what the utility requires.

Scheduled maintenance beats emergency calls

Most commercial plumbing emergencies have a warning. A floor drain that drains slowly for a month, a restroom valve that sticks, or a water heater that rumbles each morning are all signs the next failure is close. A maintenance visit catches them at a time that suits the business.

We suggest a short walk-through of the building so we know where the shutoffs are, what the water heater is, and where the cleanouts sit. That knowledge speeds up any later emergency, because we are not hunting for a valve while water is on the floor.

Rental properties and multi-tenant buildings

Landlords have a different problem than owners who live on site. A tenant may not report a slow leak until the damage is large, and a single line can serve several units. We find out which unit an issue starts in, fix the shared line, and write up the findings in a form you can pass to a manager or an insurer.

Properties near the University of West Georgia tend to turn over occupants every year, which means repeated first-time users of old plumbing. A short note to new tenants about wipes, grease, and the location of the main shutoff prevents many of the calls we get from rentals.

What to do before we arrive at your business

Tell staff which restrooms or fixtures to stop using. Close the nearest shutoff valve if water is spreading, and keep customers away from wet floors.

If a kitchen drain is backing up, stop dishwashing and do not pour chemical cleaner into the line.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. Commercial accounts add backflow testing and fixture counts, and the City of Carrollton asks commercial water customers for backflow test sheets.

What changes the price of commercial work

Commercial pricing follows the building and the scope.

  • Type of problem and its location in the building.
  • Equipment needed, such as a jetter or camera.
  • Working hours: after hours and weekends cost more.
  • Parts and fixture specifications.
  • Permit and inspection needs for larger work.

You see a clear price before work starts. Many commercial policies cover water damage but not the repair itself. Ask your agent before the next incident.
