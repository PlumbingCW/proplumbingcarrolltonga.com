Mandeville Mills and Mill Village Historic District

Plumber in Mandeville Mill Village, Carrollton, GA

The Mandeville Mills Mill Village is a historic neighborhood west of downtown Carrollton, built for textile workers in the early 1900s. This page covers what the area is, what plumbing questions homeowners and landlords there can sort out themselves, and when to call.

About the Mill Village

According to the National Park Service nomination, the Mandeville Mills and Mill Village Historic District covers about 180 acres west of downtown Carrollton, centered roughly on Aycock, Lovvorn and Burson Streets. It was listed in 2006 and its period of significance runs from 1898 to 1953. The village originally had roughly 150 houses, built in phases. The first, around 1900, was on Burson Avenue, West Broad Street and Pine Street, and a second phase in 1908 and 1909 followed on Aycock Street, the west end of Lovvorn Road and Avenues A, B and C.

The nomination says the historic street plan, lot layout and landscaping remain intact, and that four mill buildings have been converted into residential loft apartments.

House types described in the National Register record

Mill villages often have a small set of repeating house designs. The nomination names these. It describes the buildings and does not say what pipes are inside.

House type.Description from the record.
Central hallway.One room deep, with a central hall between two rooms.
Shotgun and double shotgun.One room wide and two or more rooms deep, built mainly from the 1870s to the 1920s.
Gabled ell cottage.T-shaped or L-shaped plan with a gabled roof.
Pyramid cottage.Square mass with four main rooms and a steep pyramidal roof, built in the early 1900s.
Saltbox.A New England design the nomination calls rare in Georgia and limited almost entirely to mill villages.
Bungalow.Long, low and often Craftsman-influenced, popular from 1900 to 1930.

Plumbing questions that come with small houses and rental homes

The village houses are one story with front porches, and the nomination says they include single-family and multi-family buildings. Small, simple floor plans can make plumbing easier to reach. Many fixtures share a wall, and a leak in one place can show up in the next room. We do not assume any particular problem, because each house has been maintained differently.

If you manage several houses, keeping a note of where each main shutoff and cleanout sits saves time on every call. Tenants can then close the water quickly while they wait for help.

A simple way to describe a problem on the phone

A clear description helps us tell you what to try and what to avoid.

  1. Say where it is.

    Name the room and the fixture, and say whether the water is clear or discolored.

  2. Say when it started.

    A sudden change points to a different cause than a slow decline.

  3. Say what you tried.

    Tell us if you used drain cleaner or closed a valve.

  4. Give the address.

    A street and house number let us tell you whether we can help there.

Services for homes in the Mill Village

Drains that slow or back up are a drain cleaning call, and a repeat clog in the same place is a reason to look at the line with a camera inspection. A hidden leak, such as a meter that moves when every tap is off, is found with leak detection.

For fixtures, toilet repair and faucet and sink repair cover the everyday work. If you are extending a house, ask about rough-in on the call.

Carrollton, GA note: water, sewer and who to ask

The City of Carrollton says it offers water, sewer and garbage service and lists a water billing office at 770-830-2000. The nomination places this district west of downtown, but the village was historically outside the city limits and we have not confirmed its current status. If you are unsure whether your address is on city water and sewer, check your bill or call the city before you assume.

The city also asks owners of structures built in 1989 or earlier to check their water service line. Houses of this period fall within that request.
