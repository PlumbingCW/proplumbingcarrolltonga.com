Leak emergency: confirm it and stop the water

First, look for water you can see. Signs include a wet baseboard, a growing ceiling stain, a spongy floor, or running water when every fixture is off. Any of these is a reason to close the main shutoff and call (770) 214-4545 while you wait.

Second, read the water meter. Turn off every fixture, note the reading, wait two hours without using water, and read it again. The EPA says that if the meter changes at all, you probably have a leak. Third, put a few drops of food coloring in each toilet tank. If color shows in the bowl within 10 minutes without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

Why hidden leaks cost more than they look

The EPA estimates that the average household wastes more than 9,300 gallons of water a year to leaks, and that about nine percent of homes leak 50 gallons or more per day. Many of those leaks run for months because they sit inside a wall, under a slab, or in a buried line.

Water that has nowhere to go finds soft materials. Drywall stains, flooring lifts, and mold follows. A fast, accurate find is cheaper than the damage a slow one allows.

How we find a leak without tearing things open

We use several methods and combine them, because no single tool works on every pipe.

  • Meter and pressure testing to confirm that a leak exists and whether it is on the supply or drain side.
  • Acoustic listening with ground microphones and handheld sensors on pipes and slabs.
  • Thermal imaging to spot warm hot-water lines or cool damp areas behind finishes.
  • Isolation testing, closing valves in sections to narrow the zone.
  • Tracer gas on lines where sound travels poorly, such as plastic pipe.
  • Camera inspection of drain lines when water loss may be on the sewer side.

Checking your water service line in Carrollton

The City of Carrollton is building a water service line inventory and asks owners of structures built in 1989 or earlier to check their service line material. The city's page describes a scratch test and a magnet test to tell lead, copper, galvanized steel, and plastic apart, with the line found where it enters the building near the shutoff valve.

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and eventually leaks at the threads. If your line is galvanized and you have unexplained pressure drops or discolored water, leak detection and an eventual repipe are worth discussing.

What to do while you wait for a leak check

Do not open walls or floors yourself. Write down the meter reading, note where you see damp spots, and keep fixtures off so the meter test is clean.

If water is spraying, close the main shutoff and call right away. Do not wait for a scheduled visit.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. Finding a leak needs no permit. Repairing the pipe after the wall is open may.

What changes the price of leak detection

Detection is priced by time and equipment, not by the size of the leak.

  • How many methods the job needs before the leak is located.
  • Whether the leak is inside a wall, under a slab, or buried in a yard.
  • The size and layout of the home or building.
  • Whether a camera inspection of the drain line is also needed.
  • Whether the call is urgent or after hours.
