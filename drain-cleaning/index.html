Emergency Drain Cleaning in Carrollton, GA

Signs a drain needs a plumber and not another bottle of cleaner

One slow sink is often a hair or soap clog close to the fixture. Two slow fixtures, or a tub that gurgles when the washing machine drains, point to a blockage further down the branch. A toilet that bubbles when you run the shower tells you the problem is in the main line.

Chemical drain openers are the usual first move and the usual mistake. They sit in a standing pipe, soften a small part of the clog, and leave caustic water behind for whoever opens the trap next. If a bottle did not work the first time, a second bottle will not either. Call us and we will go in mechanically instead.

Drain emergency: what to do while you wait

Stop adding water. Hold the laundry, skip the dishwasher, and ask everyone in the house to wait on showers. Every gallon that goes down a blocked line has to come back out somewhere, and it comes out of the lowest drain in the house.

Then call (770) 214-4545 and tell the dispatcher which fixtures are affected. A single backed-up sink is a branch problem. A backed-up floor drain, tub, and toilet together is a main line problem, and we send equipment for that. Move rugs and cords away from the wet area while you wait.

Cable, jet, or camera first?

Which tool opens your drain depends on what is in the pipe. Here is how we decide on a typical call.

How a drain call works

The visit follows the same order every time so nothing is guessed.

  1. Describe it.

    On the phone, tell us which fixtures are slow or backed up. That tells us whether to bring a hand tool or a main line machine.

  2. Find the access.

    We look for a cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the yard, basement, or garage. Using it keeps the machine out of your toilet.

  3. Clear and test.

    We run the cable or jet until water moves freely, then run water for several minutes to confirm the line holds.

  4. Explain the cause.

    You hear what we pulled out and whether a camera run is worth doing before the next clog.

Why drains in Carrollton homes clog again

Carrollton has a mix of newer subdivisions and houses that are decades old. The city's water service line inventory asks owners of structures built in 1989 or earlier to check their water lines, which tells you how much older housing is still in service. Older drain lines tend to have rough joints, sags, and root entry points where a cable snags and a clog restarts.

If you rent a house near the University of West Georgia, wipes and cooking grease are the two most common causes we would expect to find. Flushable wipes do not break up in a drain the way toilet paper does. Kitchen grease cools into a paste on the pipe wall and narrows it a little more with every dish load. See our notes on main sewer line cleaning if every drain in the house is slow at once.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. Clearing a clog does not usually need a permit, but replacing a trap, vent, or drain line does fall under the code.

What changes the price of a drain call

We do not quote a number sight unseen because the same symptom can come from very different causes. These are the factors that move the price.

  • How far down the line the clog sits, and whether a cleanout gives us direct access.
  • The line size and whether it is a branch drain or the main line to the street.
  • What the blockage is made of: soft debris, grease, roots, or a foreign object.
  • Whether the call is after hours, on a weekend, or on a holiday.
  • Whether a camera run is needed to find out why the line keeps clogging.

You get a clear price before work starts, and if we find something larger than a clog we explain it before going further. Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not the cost of clearing a blockage itself, so ask your insurer about your own coverage.
