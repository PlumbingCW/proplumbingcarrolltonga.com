South Carrollton Residential Historic District

Plumber in South Carrollton, Carrollton, GA

South Carrollton is a historic residential area southeast of downtown Carrollton, with houses from the 1870s to the 1930s. This page explains what the area is, what homeowners there can reasonably check in their plumbing, and when to call.

About the South Carrollton neighborhood

The South Carrollton Residential Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1984. According to the National Park Service nomination, it covers about 55 acres on gently hilly ground southeast of downtown. The boundary runs roughly along Mill Street and West Avenue to the north, Bradley Street to the west, the Central of Georgia Railroad tracks and Harrison Avenue to the south, and Garrett Street, Tillman Drive and Hill Drive to the east.

The nomination describes almost entirely single-family housing on tree-lined streets, with houses set close together on long, narrow lots. The largest homes sit along Dixie and Bradley Streets, while more modest housing lines Austin Avenue and Clifton Terrace.

What the National Park Service record says about the housing

These details come from the district nomination, not from our own surveys. They describe the buildings and say nothing about the pipes inside them.

Topic.What the record says.
Age of the houses.Built from the 1870s to the 1930s, with many smaller houses from the 1920s and 1930s built for mill employees.

What this means for plumbing questions

A house that has stood for decades has usually been updated more than once, and nobody can tell from the street what is inside the walls. We do not assume a particular pipe material or a particular problem for any house here. The honest starting point is to find out what you have, which is why a camera inspection of the drain line and a look at the visible supply pipe come before any repair plan.

If you own one of these homes, it helps to know where your main shutoff valve is, whether your water line is copper, plastic, steel or something else, and where your sewer cleanout sits. Those three facts shorten any plumbing visit.

Five things worth checking in an older house

These are general checks that apply to any older home. They are not findings about this district.

  1. Find the main shutoff.

    Locate it now and test that it turns, so you are not searching for it with water on the floor.

  2. Look at the supply pipe.

    Check the color and finish where the line enters the house.

  3. Find the cleanout.

    A capped pipe in the yard or crawlspace gives a plumber access to the sewer line.

  4. Watch the water meter.

    If it moves with every fixture off, water is going somewhere.

  5. Note slow drains.

    A drain that slows in several fixtures points to a line problem rather than one clog.

Plumbing services for South Carrollton homes

Homeowners here most often call about the same things as anywhere in Carrollton. A single slow sink or tub is a job for drain cleaning. Several fixtures backing up together usually means the main line, and a sewer camera inspection shows where and why. If the water meter keeps moving or a bill jumps, leak detection locates the leak before walls are opened.

For no hot water or a leaking tank, see water heater repair. If the supply pipes themselves have reached the end of their life, repiping is the longer project, and we can talk through the scope on a call.

Carrollton, GA note: what the city says about older homes

The City of Carrollton is building an inventory of water service lines and asks owners of structures built in 1989 or earlier to check what their service line is made of. Houses in this district were built well before that date, so the request applies to them. The city's page describes a simple scratch and magnet test,.

The city also says it issues plumbing permits and that permits are picked up in person by the state card holder. If a repair may need a permit, ask whoever does the work who will handle it.
