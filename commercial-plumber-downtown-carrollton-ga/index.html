About downtown Carrollton

According to the National Park Service nomination, the Carrollton Downtown Historic District surrounds Adamson Square. Its rough boundaries are Johnson Avenue to the north, White Street to the east, Mill Street to the south and Barnes Street to the west. It was listed in 2007, covers about 44 acres and contains 103 resources, 74 of them contributing.

The nomination calls the district overwhelmingly commercial and civic. Most buildings are one and two story brick commercial buildings that sit flush with the sidewalk. The oldest known structure is the Mandeville Building from 1873, and the bulk of the contributing properties were built from 1890 until 1917. The nomination also notes that the neighborhoods around the district are residential but fall outside its boundaries.

Commercial plumbing around the square

A building that has served shops, offices and restaurants for a century has usually been divided, combined and renovated several times. Pipes from different eras can meet in the same wall. A camera inspection of the drain line and a careful look at the supply side tell you what you have before any renovation plan is drawn up.

For tenants, the practical question is often who is responsible for what. Your lease decides that. Describe the problem when you call and tell us whether you are the owner or the tenant, so we can say what the next step is.

It also helps to say what kind of business you run, how many restrooms or kitchens are involved, and whether the problem affects one fixture or the whole building. A single dripping faucet and a floor drain that backs up during service are very different calls, and the answer shapes what we ask you to do first.

Services for downtown businesses

Our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle restrooms, kitchens and mechanical rooms. Slow or backed-up lines are handled through drain cleaning and, for stubborn grease, hydro jetting. When a line keeps failing, sewer line repair covers fixing the pipe itself.

If a leak is suspected but not visible, leak detection finds it without opening walls first, which matters in a historic building.

Carrollton, GA note: backflow and permits

The City of Carrollton says plumbing permits are issued by the city and picked up in person by the state card holder. Ask the city what applies to your building before a renovation starts, and ask whether your building has backflow assemblies that need testing.

Because the buildings are part of a National Register district, it is sensible to check with the city before changing a storefront.
