About Carrollton Pro Plumbing

We are a Carrollton plumbing line built around one idea: when water is where it should not be, a person should answer the phone and tell you what to do.

Written by the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

What we do

Carrollton Pro Plumbing takes plumbing calls for Carrollton and the towns around it, around the clock, and sends licensed plumbers to the address. Our address is 1670 US Highway 27, Carrollton, GA 30116. We cover drains, water heaters, leaks, sewer lines, fixtures, repiping, and light commercial work, and the full list is on the services page.

We take work by phone and not through web forms, because plumbing problems are easier to sort out in a short conversation than in a message box. When you call, you describe what is happening, the dispatcher tells you what to shut off or avoid, and a technician is scheduled. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

How we work

You hear the price before work starts. The technician finds the cause, explains it in plain words, and gives you the price. If the job turns out larger than expected, you hear about it before more work happens.

We do not publish prices on the website because the same symptom can have different causes. Each service page lists the factors that move the price so you know what to expect. We also say when a cheaper fix is available, such as a repair on a water heater that has years left.

How to check any plumber in Georgia

Georgia licenses plumbers as journeyman, Class I master, Class II master, and statewide master, through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers under the Secretary of State. The state's licensing portal, GOALS, is where a license can be verified. Ask any plumber you are thinking of hiring for the license information and check it.

Ask who will pull the permit when the job needs one. The City of Carrollton says permits are picked up in person by the state card holder, which means the licensed person. A plumber who avoids that question is worth a second look.

Local knowledge that shapes the work

Carrollton gets its water from the Little Tallapoosa River and three reservoirs, treated at the City of Carrollton Water Filter Plant. The city's service line inventory asks owners of houses built in 1989 or earlier to check their water lines, which shows how much older housing there is. Winter lows near 31.6 degrees in January are enough to split an unprotected pipe.

We write what we know about local conditions on the service pages, and where we do not have a verified figure, such as water hardness, we say so and tell you how to find out. If you want to see the work we do for common problems, start with drain cleaning or water heater repair.

What we do not do

We do not publish prices before we see the problem, we do not use web forms, and we do not use scare tactics to sell bigger jobs. If a repair is enough, we say so. If a replacement makes sense, we explain why and what the alternatives are.

We also do not claim credentials we cannot prove on a web page. Instead, we encourage you to check any plumber's license with the state before work starts, ours included.

Where to read more

The service pages explain each type of job, the causes we see, and the factors that move the price. The blog has longer guides on topics such as checking your water line and reading a water meter. Call (770) 214-4545 whenever you are ready to talk.

Questions we hear most

People ask whether the technician is licensed, whether the price changes on arrival, and whether we cover their town. Ask for the license and verify it with the state. The price is set after the technician finds the cause and before work starts. The service areas page lists the towns we serve, and a phone call confirms your address.

If you have another question, call. A two minute conversation answers most of them.

Why the phone, not a form

A form asks you to guess which details matter. A call lets the dispatcher ask the right questions in the right order, such as whether the water is clear or brown, whether it is hot or cold, and where it is coming from. Those answers change what the technician brings.

It also lets us tell you what to do in the first few minutes, which is often the part that limits damage the most.

Our service area in brief

We work from US Highway 27 in Carrollton and reach the city, Bowdon, and the nearby Carroll County towns listed on the service areas page.

The standard we measure work against

According to the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, the state publishes its amendments to the International Plumbing Code. That is the standard we point to for permitted work, and a licensed plumber should be able to tell you which parts apply to your job.
