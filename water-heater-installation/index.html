Water Heater Installation in Carrollton, GA

Sizing a water heater to the way your household uses it

Tank size follows household size and habits. A couple that showers in the morning rarely needs what a family of five does, and a house with a soaking tub needs a heater that can fill it without running cold. The first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label tells you how much hot water a tank delivers in a busy hour, which is a better number than raw gallons.

For a tankless unit the number that matters is flow rate at your winter inlet temperature. Water entering a house is colder in January than in August, and a heater sized for summer can fall short on the coldest mornings. We size to the cold case.

How an installation project runs

A planned install follows a fixed order, which lets us catch conflicts before the walls are closed.

  1. Walk the site.

    We look at the location, venting path, gas and electric supply, and the drain for the relief valve.

  2. Agree the plan.

    You choose the type and size. We note what needs to change, such as a new circuit or a larger gas line.

  3. Rough in and set.

    We run the new lines, set the unit on a stand or pan where needed, and connect water, fuel, and vent.

  4. Test and hand over.

    We check for leaks and combustion safety, set the temperature, and show you the shutoffs and the relief valve.

Parts of the job that get missed on a rushed install

These are the details that decide whether a new heater runs quietly for years or causes a callback.

  • A drain pan with a pipe to a safe discharge point when the heater sits above finished space.
  • A thermal expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system.
  • Dielectric unions or flexible connectors that keep copper and steel from corroding at the joint.
  • A relief valve discharge pipe that ends where it cannot scald anyone.
  • A properly pitched vent with the clearances the manufacturer lists.
  • A shutoff valve within reach of the tank so you can stop a leak fast.

Permits, inspections, and who can pull them

The City of Carrollton lists plumbing, gas, electrical, and HVAC among the permits it issues. The page states that permits are picked up in person by the state card holder, which is why a licensed contractor handles the paperwork. Georgia licenses plumbers as journeyman, Class I master, Class II master, or statewide master through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers.

Before any installer starts, ask whether the job needs a permit and who pulls it. Then confirm the plumber's license with the Georgia Secretary of State licensing portal. A legitimate contractor answers both questions without hesitation.

What to do before a water heater install

Clear the area around the old heater and tell us where the main shutoff is. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Do not light a pilot or restore power to a heater that has been disconnected or has leaked.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. A new location for a heater changes the venting, gas, drain pan, and relief valve requirements, so ask about the permit before the date is set.

What changes the price of an installation

There is no useful flat price, because the install conditions differ so much from house to house.

  • The water heater itself, including size, fuel type, and efficiency.
  • New gas piping, venting, or an electrical circuit.
  • Location: a ground-floor closet costs less than an attic platform.
  • Permit fees where they apply.
  • Haul-away of an old unit.
  • Expansion tank, pan, and drain additions.

We give you a written price before work starts so you can compare options without pressure.
