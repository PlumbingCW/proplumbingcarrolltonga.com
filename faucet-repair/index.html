Faucet emergency: water that will not stop

Look under the sink for the two small oval valves, one hot and one cold, and turn each clockwise to stop the supply to that faucet. If they will not turn, or they leak when you try, close the main shutoff for the house.

Put a bucket under the trap and towels in the cabinet. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell us whether the water is dripping from the supply line, the drain trap, or the faucet itself. That tells the technician which parts to bring.

Why faucets drip and what actually fixes it

A drip from the spout comes from a worn cartridge, washer, or seat. Water around the handle comes from a worn O-ring. Low flow from a single faucet usually traces to a clogged aerator or cartridge, not the supply line.

Cartridge faucets are repaired by replacing the cartridge. Older compression faucets need new washers and sometimes a new seat. If the faucet is more than 15 years old, the part may cost nearly as much as a new faucet, and we will say so.

What a faucet and sink visit covers

Most visits are one trip.

  1. Find the source.

    We dry everything, run the water, and watch where the first drop appears.

  2. Isolate the fixture.

    We close the stop valves. If they fail we replace them first, because a bad stop valve is a flood waiting to happen.

  3. Repair or replace.

    We fix the part or install the new faucet, supply lines, and trap.

  4. Leak check.

    We run the faucet for several minutes and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

Sink problems we also fix

A faucet is only part of a sink.

  • Slow or clogged sink drains, see our drain cleaning service.

  • Corroded or seized shutoff valves under the sink.
  • Loose sinks that have pulled away from the countertop.

Stop valves: the part nobody checks

Every sink should have two small stop valves under it. They are used rarely, so they seize. When a faucet fails and the valves will not close, the only choice is the main shutoff, which cuts water to the whole house.

We test stop valves on every faucet and sink visit. Replacing a corroded one costs little compared with a flood. If your valves have not been turned in years, turn them gently a quarter turn each way to see whether they move, and call us if they leak when touched.

Low flow and noisy pipes at one faucet

A faucet that sputters usually has debris in the aerator or a worn cartridge. Unscrew the aerator and rinse it. If the problem remains, the cartridge may be shedding bits, or the supply line is kinked.

A knocking sound when you close a faucet is water hammer, caused by a sudden stop in flow. It can loosen joints over time. A small arrestor on the line absorbs the shock, and we can add one.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. A faucet swap is simple, but moving a supply line or adding a drain connection is plumbing work the code covers.
