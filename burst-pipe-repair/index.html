Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Carrollton, GA

A burst pipe can put a large amount of water into a house in a short time. The first job is to stop the water, and the second is to repair the line. We answer burst pipe calls around the clock in Carrollton, in a freeze or any other time a pipe lets go. The 24/7 emergency line answers around the clock.

Pipe burst emergency: do these things in this order

Close the main water shutoff. It is usually near the water meter at the street, where the line enters the house, or in the basement or crawlspace. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, call the water utility and us.

Turn off the electricity to wet areas if you can do so from a dry spot. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain what is left. Move valuables out of the way, take photos for your insurer, and call (770) 214-4545. Do not use a flame or heat gun to thaw a frozen pipe.

Why pipes burst in a Georgia winter

Water expands as it freezes. A pipe does not usually burst where the ice forms. It splits where pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, attics, and garages are the most exposed.

Carrollton's January mean daily low sits near freezing, at about 31.6 degrees, and the area has seen heavy snow and ice. Carroll County's hazard data lists four winter weather events between 2015 and early 2019. Homes built for mild winters often have little pipe insulation, so a few cold nights can be enough.

What a burst pipe repair involves

The sequence is fixed.

  1. Stop the water.

    We confirm the main is off and isolate the failed section.

  2. Locate the break.

    We open the minimum area of wall, ceiling, or floor to reach the split.

  3. Replace the section.

    We cut out the damaged length and install new pipe and fittings.

  4. Pressure-test and advise.

    We restore water, test, and tell you where else the pipes are exposed.

Preventing the next freeze

When the forecast shows a hard freeze, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, let a faucet drip, and disconnect hoses from outdoor spigots. Keep the thermostat set steady, even if you are away. A stopped drip can let a freeze finish a pipe it started.

We can insulate exposed pipes, add foam covers to spigots, and install a shutoff that stops water quickly. If a pipe has frozen but not split, call us before you try to thaw it. See our leak detection page if you suspect a slow leak after a freeze.

After the water is off: drying and documenting

Once the leak is stopped, the next job is removing standing water and drying wet materials. Fans and open windows help, but wet drywall and insulation that stay damp for more than a day or two can grow mold. A water damage specialist uses meters to find hidden moisture.

Photograph everything before you move it, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and save receipts. We provide a written description of the repair, and your insurer may ask for it. Do this promptly, because policies set time limits for reporting a claim.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. An emergency stop-gap needs no permit, but a permanent repair that replaces a length of pipe follows the code.

What changes the price of a burst pipe repair

The repair varies with the pipe and the damage.

  • Pipe type and the length that must be replaced.
  • How much wall, ceiling, or floor must be opened.
  • Accessibility, such as attic or crawlspace.
  • Whether more than one section has split.
  • After-hours emergency dispatch.

A clear price comes before we cut. Homeowner insurance typically covers sudden pipe bursts and the resulting damage, so contact your insurer early. We document the repair for your claim.
