How the service list is organized

Plumbing problems fall into two groups. Some cannot wait: a split pipe, a backed-up main line, a tank leaking onto a floor. Others are planned, such as a new water heater in a remodel or a repipe before a renovation. The pages below are marked with that difference in mind, and the emergency pages open with what to do in the first few minutes.

If you are not sure which page fits, start with the symptom. Water where it should not be is covered under leak detection and burst pipe repair. A fixture that will not drain is a drain cleaning call, and several fixtures at once point to sewer line cleaning. No hot water is a water heater repair first and a replacement second.

When to pick repair, replacement, or installation

A repair fixes one failed part and costs the least. A replacement swaps the whole unit when repair no longer makes sense, such as a tank with a rusted body. An installation is a planned job, such as a new water heater installation in an addition or a tankless conversion. The service page for each fixture says which situation points to which choice.

The same logic applies to pipes. A single pinhole leak is a repair. Repeated leaks in older supply lines make repiping worth pricing, and a failing line in the yard may call for a camera inspection before anyone digs.

How to describe a problem on the phone

You do not need plumbing words. Say where the water is, how long it has been happening, and what you have already tried. A photo is not needed. The dispatcher asks a few questions and tells you what to shut off. If you can find the main shutoff valve before you call, say where it is.

If the problem is a smell of gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Plumbing comes after safety.

What to expect when the technician arrives

The technician looks at the problem, explains what they found, and gives you the price before work begins. You can say no. If the fix needs a part that is not on the truck, the technician explains the options and the timing.

After the repair, the technician shows you what changed and tells you how to avoid a repeat. If a permit is needed, they say so before the work starts.

The standard behind the work

Plumbing in Georgia is measured against the International Plumbing Code with the state amendments the Georgia Department of Community Affairs publishes. Each service page notes where that code and a permit may apply to the job.
