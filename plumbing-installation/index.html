Plumbing Installation in Carrollton, GA

Remodels, additions, and new builds all need plumbing that is planned before the walls close. We handle rough-in and fixture installs in Carrollton and Carroll County, from a bathroom refresh to a full addition.

Plan the plumbing before the drywall

Plumbing is the trade that is hardest to change after finish work. Supply lines, drain slopes, vent paths, and shutoff locations are set at rough-in. A mistake there means opening finished walls.

Bring us in when you are drawing the plan. We can tell you what a given layout does to the drain slope, where a shower valve should sit, and whether the new fixture count fits your existing lines.

Installation work we handle

The scope runs from one fixture to a whole addition.

  • Bathroom rough-in and fixture sets: tub, shower, toilet, vanity.
  • Kitchen sinks, faucets, dishwashers, and disposals, see garbage disposal repair and installation.
  • Water heater installs and moves, see water heater installation.
  • Laundry rough-in and washing machine boxes.
  • Outdoor spigots and hose bibs.
  • Water treatment and filtration connections.
  • Gas line connections for appliances, by a licensed technician.

How an installation job runs

Good installs follow a clear order.

  1. Review the plan.

    We read your drawings or sketch the scope with you.

  2. Rough-in.

    We set supply, drain, and vent lines before walls are closed.

  3. Test.

    We pressure-test supply lines and fill-test drains before inspection and cover-up.

  4. Finish.

    After tile and cabinets, we set fixtures and connect everything.

Permits and licensing in Carrollton

The City of Carrollton issues building, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, gas, and pool permits, and its page says permits are picked up in person by the state card holder. That means the person with the state license, not an agent. Ask any plumber who will pull the permit.

Georgia plumbing licenses include journeyman, Class I master, Class II master, and statewide master, issued through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers. The Secretary of State licensing portal lets you verify a license. We encourage you to check anyone you hire.

Choosing fixtures that will not give you trouble

Fixture choice affects the plumbing under the surface. A wall-mounted faucet needs a different rough-in than a deck-mounted one. A freestanding tub needs a floor drain location fixed before the slab or subfloor is finished. A rain shower head wants more flow than an old supply line may carry.

Bring us fixture model numbers early. Manufacturers publish rough-in dimensions, and we check them against your framing. A fixture bought on sale before it is checked can cost more to fit than the saving.

What to expect during a remodel

Water is usually shut off for part of a day while we tie into existing lines. We tell you the schedule in advance so you can plan around it. Where the work blocks a working bathroom, we try to get one running again by the end of each day.

Dust, noise, and open walls are part of rough-in. Once the pressure test passes and any required inspection is complete, the walls can be closed. Finish plumbing happens after tile, cabinets, and paint, so we usually visit twice.

Water treatment and filtration

The City of Carrollton publishes an annual water quality report from its water filter plant. If you want extra filtration for taste or a specific concern, a point-of-use filter at the kitchen sink or a whole-house system ahead of the water heater can be installed as part of a project. Match the filter to what you want to remove, and check that it is certified for that purpose.

A softener or filter also changes the plumbing around it. It needs a bypass, a drain, and a spot with power and room for salt. We lay this out with you before the walls close.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. New rough-in work is where the code matters most, because pipe sizing, venting, and slope are checked before walls close.

What changes the price of an installation

Installation pricing follows scope and conditions.

  • Number and type of fixtures.
  • Whether walls, floors, or slab must be opened.
  • Distance from existing supply and drain lines.
  • Fixture quality you choose.
  • Permit and inspection needs.
  • Access and site conditions.

A clear price comes before work starts. If your project includes old pipes that need replacing, ask about repiping at the same time.
