Sink Plumbing Installation: A Carrollton, GA Homeowner Guide

A sink looks simple from above, but underneath it needs hot and cold supply lines, a trap, a vent, and a drain that slopes the right way. This guide explains what goes into a sink installation so you can plan a kitchen or bath project and know what to ask a plumber.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

What a sink needs behind the wall

Every sink needs five things: hot water, cold water, a drain, a trap, and a vent. The supply lines come from the wall or the floor through stop valves. The drain leaves the sink through a tailpiece, passes through the trap, which holds water to block sewer gas, and enters the drain line in the wall.

The vent lets air into the drain system so the water flows freely and the trap does not siphon dry. A sink that gurgles is often a vent problem, not a clog. In a new layout, the vent path decides where the sink can go.

Parts list for a typical installation

These are the pieces we bring or confirm before a sink goes in.

  • Faucet with its mounting hardware and supply lines.
  • Two quarter-turn stop valves, one hot and one cold.
  • A drain assembly, basket strainer for a kitchen or a pop-up for a bath.
  • A P-trap sized to the drain, with slip nuts and washers.
  • Plumber's putty or silicone for the drain flange, as the manufacturer directs.
  • Sealant and mounting clips for an undermount sink.

Kitchen sink and bathroom sink compared

The two share parts but differ in a few important ways.

How a sink installation runs

The work is quick when the rough-in is already right.

  1. Confirm the rough-in.

    We check supply and drain positions against the new sink and faucet.

  2. Set the sink and faucet.

    The faucet goes on before the sink is installed, which is easier than reaching under later.

  3. Connect supply and drain.

    We attach stop valves, supply lines, and trap, and tighten each joint.

  4. Test for leaks.

    We run water for several minutes and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

Common problems in a DIY sink install

A trap installed backward holds no water seal. A trap arm that slopes up toward the wall holds water and clogs. Supply lines tightened with a wrench instead of by hand plus a quarter turn can crack the fitting. Plumber's tape on compression fittings does nothing useful and can keep them from seating.

Faucets sold with plastic mounting nuts need careful tightening. Too loose and the faucet wobbles, too tight and the nut cracks. If the sink is a heavy cast iron or stone type, support it correctly. A sink that sags pulls on the drain and cracks the connection.

Planning the layout before you buy

Pick the faucet and sink together and check the hole count, because a three-hole faucet will not fit a one-hole sink. Measure the cabinet opening and the depth under the sink so the trap and any disposal fit. Take a photo of the existing plumbing before you start.

If you are moving the sink more than a few feet, call a plumber. The drain slope and vent rules limit where the sink can go, and the supply lines need to reach. Planning before you demolish saves a lot of rework. See our disposal service if the sink includes a disposer.

Mistakes that cause leaks after an install

Over-tightening plastic nuts cracks them and under-tightening leaves a slow weep. Skipping the plumber's putty or gasket on the drain flange lets water seep into the cabinet. Reusing old supply lines can pass a leak that was waiting to start.

After the install, run the water for several minutes, fill the sink and drain it, and check every joint with a dry paper towel. Check again the next day, since slow leaks take time to show.

Carrollton, GA note: permits, licenses, and who does the work

A like-for-like sink swap often needs no permit, but moving a drain or adding fixtures can. According to the City of Carrollton, it issues plumbing permits, and permits are picked up in person by the state card holder. If your project changes the layout, ask the city building office.

Georgia licenses plumbers through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and the Secretary of State's licensing portal lets you confirm a license. Hire only someone you have checked.
