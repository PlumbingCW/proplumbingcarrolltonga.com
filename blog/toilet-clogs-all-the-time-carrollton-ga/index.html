Toilet troubleshooting

Toilet Clogs All the Time: A Carrollton, GA Homeowner Guide

A toilet that clogs once is a nuisance. One that clogs every week has a cause that plunging will not cure. The cause can be the toilet, the pipe under the floor, the vent, or what is being flushed. This guide helps you work out which, and when to call.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

Start with the simple causes

Too much paper, flushable wipes, and cotton products cause most clogs. Wipes sold as flushable do not break up like toilet paper, and they snag in the trap or the line. If your household uses them, switch to the trash can and see whether the problem stops.

A weak flush is the next suspect. If the bowl does not empty with force, the rim jets may be blocked with mineral deposits, or the water level in the tank may be low. A weak flush cannot clear even normal waste.

Symptoms and likely causes

Where else the problem shows up tells you where the clog sits.

What to try before calling

These are safe steps for most homes.

  1. Use the right plunger.

    A flange plunger with a rubber flap fits a toilet better than a flat cup plunger. Cover the opening and push steadily.

  2. Try a closet auger.

    A short auger made for toilets reaches the trap without scratching the bowl.

  3. Check the water level.

    The tank should fill to the mark. A low tank gives a weak flush.

  4. Clean the rim jets.

    Mineral buildup in the small holes under the rim weakens the flush. A wire or a descaling solution clears them.

When the toilet is the problem

Some older toilets use trapways that clog easily, especially low-flow models from the early years of those designs. If you have plunged the same toilet for years, replacement may be the practical fix. A modern toilet with a smooth trapway handles more with less water.

If you rent near campus or have many occupants, usage can overwhelm a standard toilet. A pressure-assisted model or a more powerful flush design helps in those cases. We cover installs on our toilet repair and installation page.

When the clog is not in the toilet

A toilet that clogs and then backs up a shower or floor drain has a blockage in the line past the toilet. Roots, grease, and collapsed sections are possible. A camera run shows which. If the main line is the problem, no change to the toilet will solve it.

Frequent clogs can also mean a venting problem. The vent lets air into the drain so waste flows. A blocked vent makes toilets gurgle and drain slowly. That is a plumber's diagnosis, not a plunger's.

Habits that cause repeat clogs

Wipes marked flushable are a leading cause, since they do not break down the way paper does. Too much paper at once, feminine products, and cotton balls cause the same trouble. Keep a wastebasket in the bathroom and tell guests where it is.

Low-flow toilets work well but leave less margin when the bowl is overloaded. If you have an older low-flow model that clogs weekly, the trap may be partly blocked by mineral scale, and a replacement may be the cleaner fix.

Signs the problem is deeper

If water rises in a tub or shower when you flush, or a floor drain gurgles, the blockage is past the toilet. A single toilet in the farthest bathroom that clogs repeatedly may sit on a branch with a belly in the pipe. A camera shows which.

If the toilet rocks, the floor is soft, or you smell sewer gas around the base, the seal or the flange may have failed. Stop using the toilet and call, since a bad seal lets water into the subfloor.

Carrollton, GA note: city sewer and who to ask

The City of Carrollton states on its utilities page that it offers water, sewer, and garbage services to its citizens. If your house is on city sewer and the blockage is in the public main, the utility handles that part. The line from your house to the connection is generally your responsibility, but where the boundary sits differs by utility, so ask.

Some properties near Carrollton use septic systems rather than city sewer. A septic system has its own rules about what can go down the drain. Tell the plumber which system you have when you call.
