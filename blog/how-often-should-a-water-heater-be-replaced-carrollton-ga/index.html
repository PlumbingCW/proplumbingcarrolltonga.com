Water heater guide

How Often Should a Water Heater Be Replaced in Carrollton, GA?

Most storage tank water heaters last 10 to 15 years, according to the Department of Energy, and tankless units can last more than 20. The number on the calendar matters less than the signs the tank is giving you. This guide covers how to read your tank's age, what failure looks like, and when replacement beats another repair.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

The short answer on lifespan

The Department of Energy's consumer guidance says most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, while tankless models can last more than 20. Those ranges are averages. A tank in a conditioned closet with a well-kept anode rod can outlast a tank in a hot attic. A unit in a house with heavy mineral buildup can fail early.

Treat the range as a planning window, not a deadline. At 10 years, start watching. At 12, start pricing a replacement. At 15, expect the next serious repair to be the last one.

How to find out how old your tank is

The rating plate on the side of the tank lists the model and serial number. Most manufacturers encode the manufacture date in the serial number, usually as a letter for the month and two digits for the year. The code layout varies by brand, so a quick search of the brand's own documentation tells you how to read it.

If the plate is unreadable, ask the plumber to check on a visit. We read tank dates routinely and can tell you how many years you likely have left, given what we see in the tank, the fittings, and the base.

Warning signs and what they usually mean

Some symptoms call for a repair. Others say the tank is finished.

Sign.What it usually means.Typical next step.
Rusty or brown hot water, cold water clear.Tank interior corroding.Plan to replace.
Popping or rumbling on heat-up.Sediment baked on the bottom.Flush, and if it recurs, plan to replace.

Why planned beats emergency

A tank rarely fails politely. It leaks at night or gives out the morning guests arrive. A planned replacement means you pick the model, compare quotes, and schedule the work. An emergency one means you take what is in stock and pay for the haste.

If your tank is past 10 years and in a spot where a leak would damage finished rooms, such as a closet above living space, ask for a drain pan with a pipe to a safe discharge point at the next visit. A pan costs little and can turn a flood into a puddle.

Extending the life of the tank you have

Set the thermostat at 120 degrees, which the Department of Energy calls safe for most people. A lower setting means less stress on the tank and less scale. Check the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year by lifting the lever briefly and making sure water runs out and stops when you let go.

If you know your water leaves deposits on fixtures, consider an annual flush to clear sediment from the bottom of the tank. The anode rod is the sacrificial part that protects the steel. When it is eaten away, the tank starts to corrode. Checking it every few years can add useful life.

Gas, electric, and what the choice does to lifespan

Gas and electric tanks both fall in the same general lifespan range. The failure modes differ. An electric tank's heating elements sit in the water and wear out or scale over, and they can be replaced without replacing the tank. A gas tank's burner, thermocouple, and gas valve wear out separately, and the flue gives a second place to rust.

That difference changes the repair math. An electric tank with a failed element is often a good repair even at an advanced age, as long as the tank itself is sound. A gas tank with a failed gas control valve on a 12 year old unit is usually a replacement, because the part costs a large share of a new tank.
