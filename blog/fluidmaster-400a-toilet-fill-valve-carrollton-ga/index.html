Toilet repair guide

Fluidmaster 400A Toilet Repair Valve Guide for Carrollton, GA

The Fluidmaster 400A is a universal fill valve sold to replace the part inside the tank that refills the toilet after each flush. When a toilet runs, hisses, or refills on its own, the fill valve or the flapper is usually to blame. This guide walks through when the 400A is the right fix and how to install it.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

When a fill valve is the problem

A fill valve controls how water enters the tank. When it wears out, it can fail to shut off, which sends water down the overflow tube. You hear a hiss or a trickle long after the flush, and the toilet may refill on its own every few minutes.

The flapper is the other common cause. To tell them apart, close the supply valve and watch the water level. If the level drops from the flapper leaking, the problem is the flapper. If the valve is the problem, the water will rise too high or keep running when the supply is open.

Replacing the valve

Follow the instructions on the package, because the 400A has a few adjustment points. This is the general sequence.

  1. Shut off and drain.

    Close the supply valve behind the toilet, flush, and sponge out the remaining water.

  2. Remove the old valve.

    Disconnect the supply line, loosen the nut under the tank, and lift out the old valve.

  3. Set the new valve.

    Install the new valve with its washer and nut, and set the height so the critical level mark sits above the top of the overflow tube.

  4. Reconnect and adjust.

    Attach the refill tube, connect the supply, turn the water on, and adjust the float until the water stops at the right level.

Fill valve or flapper: how to decide

A short test saves a trip to the store.

Hiss after the tank fills.Fill valve.Water never shuts off when the level is correct.

Mistakes that bring the leak back

Setting the valve too tall puts water into the overflow tube. Over-tightening plastic nuts cracks the tank. A supply line that is too short, kinked, or old can leak when the valve is changed. Replacing a braided line at the same time costs very little and avoids a second visit.

If your toilet is old and the tank is cracked, or the bolts that hold the tank to the bowl are corroded, a new valve will not fix it. In that case replacing the toilet makes more sense. See our toilet repair and installation page for that decision.

Fixing the problem the first time

Before you buy anything, take the tank lid off and look. Check that the chain on the flapper is not too tight or too loose, that the flapper seats cleanly, and that the float arm is not rubbing the tank wall. Many running toilets are fixed by a quarter turn of an adjustment or a clean flapper seat.

If you decide to replace the fill valve, replace the flapper in the same visit. Both are inexpensive, and they wear at the same rate. Doing only one means the toilet may still run, and you pay for a second trip to the store or a second call.

When the toilet is the real problem

A fill valve cannot fix a cracked tank, a worn tank-to-bowl gasket, or a wobbling base. If water appears around the base of the toilet after a flush, the wax ring may have failed. If the tank sweats and drips, the room may be humid, not the toilet leaking.

Older toilets also use more water per flush and clog more often. If you keep chasing small problems on a toilet that is decades old, a new one may be the practical answer.

Carrollton, GA note: why a running toilet shows up on the bill

The City of Carrollton's utilities page states that it provides water, sewer, and garbage service, and that billing questions go to the water billing office at 770-830-2000. A toilet that leaks a little all day adds up on a metered bill. The EPA says a toilet leak can be found with a dye test, where color appears in the bowl within 10 minutes.

If your bill jumps and the dye test is clean, call the utility to ask about your meter and consider leak detection. We cover that on our leak detection page.
