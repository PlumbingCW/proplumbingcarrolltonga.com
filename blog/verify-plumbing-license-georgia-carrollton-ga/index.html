How to Check a Plumber's License in Georgia, for Carrollton Homeowners

Before a stranger works on your plumbing, you should know they hold the right license. Georgia licenses plumbers through a state division, and the check takes a few minutes. This guide covers the license classes, how to verify one, and the questions worth asking any plumber before work starts, including us.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

Who licenses plumbers in Georgia

The Georgia Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers issues plumbing licenses. Its frequently asked questions page lists four license types: Journeyman Plumber, Class I Master Plumber, Class II Master Plumber, and Statewide Master Plumber.

In general, a journeyman works under a master, and a master plumber can run a plumbing business and pull permits. The classes differ in the type of work they cover, so ask any plumber which license they hold and what it allows.

The four license types

The state names these four. Ask which one your plumber holds.

License.Who typically holds it.Why it matters to you.
Journeyman Plumber.A plumber who works under a master.Should be supervised, not working a job alone as the business.
Class I Master Plumber.A master plumber under the state's Class I rules.Can lead a plumbing business within the scope of the class.
Class II Master Plumber.A master plumber under the state's Class II rules.Can lead a plumbing business within the scope of the class.
Statewide Master Plumber.A master plumber with statewide authority.Can work across the state under the license.

How to check a license

The state's page says licenses are handled through its licensing portal, called GOALS.

  1. Ask for the license class and number.

    Any licensed plumber gives it without hesitation.

  2. Confirm with the state.

    Use the Secretary of State's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, through its GOALS portal, to verify the license is active. The state's page points to GOALS for license verification.

  3. Match the name.

    The license should be in the name of the person or company on your contract.

  4. Ask about insurance.

    Request proof of general liability coverage if the job is large.

Questions to ask before any job

Who will do the work, and who holds the license? Will a permit be needed, and who will pull it? What is the price, and what could change it? What does the warranty cover, and for how long? Get the answers in writing.

Be wary of a plumber who wants payment up front for the whole job, will not give a written price, or says no permit is ever needed. A reputable plumber explains what the job needs and does not mind being asked.

What this means for our own work

We dispatch licensed plumbers, and we encourage you to ask for the license details of whoever arrives and to verify them with the state. We do not publish a license number on this site because the technician who comes to your door is the person to ask.

If you are planning a larger project, such as repiping or a water heater installation, ask about permits at the start and confirm who will handle the paperwork.

Red flags when hiring

Watch for a quote that is far below the others, a request for cash only, pressure to decide on the spot, or reluctance to put anything in writing. Be careful with door-to-door offers after a storm or a freeze.

A good plumber explains what they found, shows you the problem when they can, and gives you options. If you are unsure, take a day, call another company, and compare the scope and the price.

What to keep after the job

Keep the written price, the invoice, any permit paperwork, and the warranty terms. If the work needed a permit, ask for the final sign-off. Photos of the finished work help if a question comes up later.

These records matter when you sell the house, when you file an insurance claim, and when something needs to be fixed under warranty.

What to do if a license does not check out

If the name, class, or status does not match what you were told, ask the plumber to explain before work starts. Do not sign a contract or pay a deposit until it is resolved. You can ask the state division for guidance about a particular license.

If you have already hired someone and have doubts, pause the work, document what has been done, and talk with the state and with another licensed plumber about next steps.

Carrollton, GA note: permits and the state card holder

According to its licenses and permits page, the City of Carrollton issues building, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, gas, and pool permits. It adds that all permits must be picked up in person by the state card holder. That means the person with the state license must pick up the permit, not an agent or the homeowner.

The page does not spell out plumbing-specific requirements for each job, so ask the city what your project needs before work starts. If your home is outside the city limits, ask the county.
