Leak-finding guide

How to Read Your Water Meter to Find a Leak in Carrollton, GA

Your water meter is the cheapest leak detector you own. If it moves when no water is in use, water is going somewhere. This guide shows how to find the meter, run the two-hour test the EPA recommends, and narrow the leak down before you call a plumber.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

Find and read the meter

The meter is usually in a box near the street, with a lid you can lift. Some are in a basement or crawlspace. Inside, a dial shows the total water used, and many meters have a small leak indicator, a triangle or a star that spins when any water flows.

Write down the number on the register. Some meters read in gallons and some in cubic feet, so note which. If you cannot find the meter, call the water utility. Do not open any box with signs of animals or damage, and watch for insects.

The two-hour leak test

The EPA describes this simple test. It works for most houses.

  1. Stop all water use.

    Turn off faucets, the dishwasher, the washing machine, and the ice maker. Make sure no one flushes.

  2. Record the reading.

    Note the meter register and the position of the leak indicator.

  3. Wait two hours.

    Do not use any water during this time.

  4. Read it again.

    If the number changed, or the leak indicator moved, you probably have a leak.

What the result tells you

A moving meter is the start of the search, not the end.

Result.Meaning.Next step.
Meter did not move.No leak on the supply side right now.Look at the toilets, bills, and irrigation.
Meter moved slowly.A small leak, such as a toilet flapper.Run the dye test, see toilet repair.
Meter moved steadily.A larger leak in a line, a fixture, or the yard.Close the main valve and see if the meter stops.
Stops when the house main is closed.The leak is inside the house.Call for leak detection.
Keeps moving with the main closed.The leak is in the service line before the shutoff.Call the utility and a plumber.

Narrowing it down

Close the main shutoff and watch the meter. If the meter keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. Next, test the toilets with a few drops of food coloring in each tank. The EPA says color in the bowl within 10 minutes means a leak.

Check outdoor spigots, irrigation valves, and the water heater's relief valve. A wet spot on the lawn with no rain suggests a line leak. If the leak is under the floor, the meter test is the first step, and our slab leak repair page covers what follows.

Why this matters

According to the EPA, the average household wastes more than 9,300 gallons of water per year to leaks, and a family of four using more than 12,000 gallons a month may have serious leaks. A small leak is a small bill. A big hidden leak is a bill and a damage problem.

If your bill has jumped and the meter test is clean, the cause may be seasonal use or a billing issue. Call the water billing office and ask them to confirm the meter reading.

Common hidden leaks the meter will reveal

Toilets are the most common source, because a worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl without a sound. Irrigation systems come next, since a stuck valve or a cracked line can run for days unnoticed. Water heaters can leak at the relief valve or the tank connections.

Underground lines between the meter and the house are harder to spot. A soft patch of lawn, an unusually green stripe, or sound of water in the walls with every fixture off are all clues worth acting on.

Keeping a simple record

Write down the meter reading at the same time each week for a month. A normal household shows a steady pattern tied to your habits. A jump with no change in routine is the sign to run the two-hour test.

Share the readings with a plumber if you call. They narrow the search quickly and spare you from paying for equipment time you do not need.

Carrollton, GA note: who to call at the utility

The City of Carrollton's utilities page lists a water billing office at 770-830-2000 for account updates and billing concerns. If your bill looks wrong, that is the office to ask about meter readings.

If you live outside the city limits, your water may come from a different provider. Check your bill for the name and phone number of the utility that serves you before you call.
