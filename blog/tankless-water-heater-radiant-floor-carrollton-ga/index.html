Tankless guide

Tankless Water Heater for Radiant Floor Heat in Carrollton, GA

Some tankless water heaters can supply both hot water and a radiant floor loop, and some cannot. The difference is whether the unit is rated for space heating and how the floor loop is connected. This guide explains the common setups and why a designer should approve the plan before anyone buys equipment.

By the Carrollton Pro Plumbing team

Call (770) 214-4545

Two jobs, one heater

A tankless water heater has one job in most houses: heating fresh water for taps. A radiant floor has a different job, which is circulating warm water through tubing in the floor. Combining the two means one heater serves the taps and the floor, which saves equipment but needs careful design.

Not every tankless heater can do this. Some are rated for domestic water only and do not carry approval for space heating. Using one for a radiant loop without the maker's approval can void the warranty and shorten the heater's life.

Common ways to connect a radiant floor

The connection method matters more than the brand.

Setup.How it works.Watch for.
Closed loop with a heat exchanger.The heater warms water for taps and, through a separate exchanger, a sealed floor loop.Needs the maker's approval and a proper exchanger.
Dedicated boiler for the floor.A separate heat source serves only the floor.Extra equipment and space.
Open loop, potable water through floor tubing.Fresh water circulates through the floor.Many manufacturers and designers avoid this because stagnant water sits in the loop.

What the designer needs to know

A radiant floor design starts with the building, not the heater.

  • The heat loss of the room or house, which sets how much heat the floor must deliver.
  • The floor covering, since tile conducts heat better than thick carpet.
  • The tubing layout and the loop lengths.
  • The tankless unit's rating at your winter inlet water temperature.
  • The gas supply size and venting for the larger load.
  • Controls, including a thermostat, a circulator, and a mixing arrangement.

Is it a good idea in west Georgia?

Georgia winters are shorter and milder than in colder states. January lows near Carrollton average about 31.6 degrees, which is enough to want warm floors in a bathroom or basement but not enough to justify a large whole-house radiant system in most cases. A small loop for a bath or kitchen floor is a more common fit.

That changes the sizing. A single room needs far less heat than a whole house, and a tankless unit chosen for the taps may handle a small loop without strain. A whole-house system is a larger project and needs a designer.

What we can help with, and what we send to a designer

We can install and service the tankless heater, the gas line, the venting, and the plumbing connections. The radiant loop design, including heat-loss calculation and tubing layout, belongs to a designer or a heating contractor with experience in hydronic systems. We coordinate with them so the pieces fit.

If your project is a remodel, bring in the plumber and the designer early. Changing floor height or gas line size after the slab is poured is costly. See water heater installation for how we plan a new unit.

Questions to settle before any work starts

What is the heat load of the floor in the coldest weather, and does the tankless unit have the capacity to meet it plus the hot water demand at the same time? Who sizes the loop and the pump? Is the unit rated by the manufacturer for space heating? Which warranty terms change if it is used that way?

These questions decide whether the project works. A heater rated for domestic hot water may be a poor fit for a large heated slab, and a heating designer can answer the sizing question with real numbers for your house.

Maintenance if you go ahead

Systems that serve both jobs see more heat cycles, so scale builds faster. Plan for a descaling flush on a regular schedule, and ask the installer to put isolation valves on the unit. Keep the maker's manual and the commissioning notes in one place.

Check the water quality on the closed heating loop too. A loop that takes on air or loses pressure points to a leak or a failed expansion tank.

Carrollton, GA note: permits for mixed trades

According to the City of Carrollton, it issues building, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, gas, and pool permits, and permits are picked up in person by the state card holder. A tankless heater feeding a radiant floor touches plumbing, gas, and heating work, so more than one permit may apply.

Ask the building office which permits your project needs before any contractor starts. The rules depend on whether the home is inside the city limits and on the scope of work.
