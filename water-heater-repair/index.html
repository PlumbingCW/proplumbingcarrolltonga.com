Emergency Water Heater Repair in Carrollton, GA

A cold shower is an inconvenience. Water on the floor under the tank is an emergency. We repair gas and electric tank water heaters in Carrollton and tell you plainly when the tank is past fixing and replacement makes more sense. The 24/7 line is answered day and night if the tank is leaking.

Water heater emergency: shut it down first

Find the cold water supply valve on the pipe entering the top of the tank and turn it clockwise until it stops. That stops new water from feeding the leak. Then switch off the power: the breaker for an electric unit, or the gas control knob set to off for a gas unit.

Do not open a hot tap to drain it, and do not touch anything electrical while standing in water. If the water is spreading toward outlets or a finished room, call the emergency line at (770) 214-4545 and say so. A leak from a fitting on top is often a repair. A leak from the bottom of the tank usually means the steel has failed.

What the symptom usually means

No hot water at all on an electric heater often comes down to a tripped high-limit switch or a failed heating element. On a gas heater it is usually the pilot, the thermocouple, or the gas control valve. Lukewarm water that runs out quickly can mean a failed lower element or a dip tube that has broken and is mixing cold water into the top of the tank.

Rumbling or popping noises come from sediment baked onto the bottom of the tank. A rotten-egg smell is a chemical reaction in the tank between the anode rod and bacteria in the water. If you smell gas rather than sulfur, leave the house and call the gas utility first. Then call us.

What a repair visit covers

We start with the cheapest explanation and work toward the expensive one, so you only pay for the part that actually failed.

  • Testing the heating elements, thermostats, and high-limit switch on electric units.
  • Checking the pilot assembly, thermocouple, burner, and gas control on gas units.
  • Inspecting the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • Looking at the anode rod, which sacrifices itself to protect the tank steel.
  • Flushing sediment when the tank is sound and the buildup is the cause.
  • Looking at the supply connections, shutoff valve, and drain valve for slow leaks.

Repair or replace?

Two numbers decide most calls: the age of the tank and where the water is coming from. The Department of Energy says most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years.

Parts we look at on a Carrollton water heater

Georgia summers run warm enough that many water heaters sit in garages and closets with little ventilation. That heat is hard on thermostats and controls on the top of the tank. Crawlspaces and unfinished basements add damp air that rusts fittings and the bottom of the jacket.

The Department of Energy recommends a 120 degree setting as safe for most people, and a tank held at that setting works its elements and burner less. Insulating the first few feet of hot water pipe also trims standby heat loss. We check the thermostat setting on every repair call and show you where it sits.

Code and permits for this work

Georgia's Department of Community Affairs publishes the state's amendments to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the standard that plumbing work in Georgia is measured against, and the City of Carrollton issues plumbing permits through its permits office. Swapping an element or a thermostat is routine, while changes to venting, gas piping, or the relief valve discharge are code items.

What changes the price of a water heater repair

A repair quote depends on which part failed and how easy the unit is to reach.

  • The part itself: an element or thermocouple costs far less than a gas control valve.
  • Gas or electric, and the tank size.
  • Whether the unit sits in a tight closet, attic, or crawlspace.
  • Whether the call is after hours or on a weekend.
  • Age of the tank, because an old tank may not be worth a major part.

We give you a clear price before work starts, and we will say so if replacement is the better spend. Homeowner insurance often covers sudden water damage from a failed tank but not the tank itself, so check your policy.
